- 2026-09-11T16:21Z DANIEL'S FEEDBACK, 2026-09-11, verbatim: 'citing a credential is not enough. It has to be *presenting* the credential in a way that shows that the presenter is the issuee of that credential.'

This overturns the rule I had proposed for beat 15 and was about to implement. That rule was: an endorsement fills a slot if its issuer is the slotted endorser OR it cites a qualification whose issuee is the slotted endorser. His objection goes to the word 'cites': naming a SAID in an edge is a claim anybody can make, and the fold must key on a demonstration rather than on an assertion. DEBATE THIS WITH HIM BEFORE IMPLEMENTING — do not take the narrow reading and move on.

Why he is right, as far as I can see, and where it reaches further than beat 15.

Under the refusal-at-commitment reading (this.i @x7crwavm, which he ruled tentatively and asked to be watched), edge validation runs in the constructor and an endorsement whose DI2I edge fails is never committed. That guards every record utina's own constructor writes. It guards nothing about a record a stranger hands us: the check leaves no committed trace, so a later reader re-folding the log sees an endorsement carrying an edge that names the seat credential and has no way to tell whether the relation ever held. The fold cannot re-run the check — no plane above the substrate may import a KERI library — so accepting the citation means trusting the writer, and 'a stranger holding the logs recomputes the same Constitution' is the claim the whole demo rests on. A hand-built record could put an unentitled endorsement in the seat's slot and this fold would count it.

So his correction is the discomfort the refusal reading was supposed to be watched for, arriving on schedule, and the two questions are one question: what does the RECORD carry that lets a stranger see the presenter was entitled? Candidates to put to him:

(1) The evidence reading he ruled against. The constructor commits the validation result as a typed fact, and the fold consumes it, which is literally what :1430-1434 says ('that check is evidence the fold consumes, never a verdict'). Costs: the fold now leans on a fact it cannot recompute, which is a different flavour of trusting the writer — better, because the fact is committed, signed and attributable, and worse, because it is the writer's word in the bytes rather than derivable from them.

(2) The presentation discipline proper. An endorsement offered as the seat's is accompanied by a presentation of the seat credential in which the presenter demonstrates entitlement — in ACDC terms the IPEX/disclosure path, where the presenting party signs the presentation under the AID the edge constrains. Then the record carries the presentation and the demonstration is in the bytes. Costs: real machinery, and the demo is three weeks out.

(3) Narrow beat 15 out. Drop the delegated-device stratum and let the seat sign for itself, so the only slot-filling rule is exact-issuer identity and the question does not arise. Beat 15 is a leave-behind row and the script marks it cuttable. Cheapest, and it gives up the most KERI-native beat in the demo.

My own lean, stated so he can push on it: (3) for the demo and (2) as the honest destination, with (1) rejected because 'the writer says it validated' is the one shape that looks like verification and is not. But I have been wrong about this pair once already today, which is why it is a tick and not a commit.

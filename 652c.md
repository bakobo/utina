# A revoked seat credential does not unseat the seat: an endorser who cites nothing is never qualified at all
kind: todo
created: 2026-09-11T22:23Z

- 2026-09-11T22:23Z 2026-09-11, STOPPED ON A FORK before building beats 22-23. For Daniel, because it decides whether Act III has teeth and whether Act IV can be built as scripted.

MEASURED, on the record as it stands (facade, after beat 16 revokes the seat credential):
  registry state of the seat credential: revoked
  seat endorses the Q3 budget CITING the revoked credential -> COMMITTED (verify_edges checks the DI2I relation, not registry state), and the fold then declines to count it, so the slot stays pending
  seat endorses the Q3 budget citing NOTHING                -> the slot fills, and the finding is AFFIRMED

So revoking the seat credential does not unseat the seat. fold/slots.py _fills short-circuits when the actor IS the slot's endorser, and _qualified is trivially true for an act that cites nothing (slots.py:343-345 — 'an act that cites nothing is qualified trivially'). A citation is the endorser's own claim and is optional, so the check on it is optional too. An unentitled stranger cannot exploit this, because the law does not slot them; a slotted party whose credential has been revoked can.

WHY IT MATTERS TWICE OVER.
(1) Beat 17 reads 'seat 3 unfilled' only because the record has the seat not acting. Nothing stops it acting. A viewer who asks 'what happens if seat 3 endorses anyway?' gets affirmed, and Act III becomes theatre.
(2) Beat 22 needs seat 3 to endorse the SECOND amendment at 1.0 — after beat 16 revoked its credential. Under the current engine that works. Under any fix that makes revocation bite, it does not, and the script has Act III revoking a seat that Act IV then has voting. That tension is in the SCRIPT, not only in the code.

THE READINGS.
(A) Leave it. The seat's authority to fill its slot is the law naming it; the seat credential governs CITATIONS only, which is what beats 18-19 are actually about, and the revocation's real teeth are on delegates (the device's grant) and on new citations. Coherent, and it is what the script already assumes at beat 22. Cost: the credential custos-4.2.md:1420-1425 calls standing-conferring confers no standing the fold checks unless someone volunteers to cite it.

(B) A slot names the qualification its endorser must hold, as committed law. Section 9 already delegates exactly this to the domain — 'which schemas, issued by which registries, confer which powers' (custos-4.2.md:1924) — and a slot already names the schema its EVIDENCE must satisfy (@z373ew7j). Add a second term: the schema the endorser's own standing credential must satisfy, absent for Marta and Dev, GCD for the seat. Then _fills requires a standing GCD for that endorser and the citation stops being load-bearing. This is the reading that matches Daniel's own framing of the GCD ruling — 'a seat ... exists because it gets a GCD credential that says what authority it has inherited' — and it makes beat 17 mean something. Cost: every clause's bytes move again, and beat 22 needs Acme to reissue a seat credential between 17 and 22, which is an extra beat the script does not have.

(C) My reading is wrong and the seat credential was never meant to gate the seat's own acts, only to be the far node a delegate's DI2I edge resolves against. Then the name 'standing-conferring' is the problem and the span to argue from is 1420-1425.

MY LEAN, stated so he can push on it: (B) is right in principle and (A) is right for 2026-09-27. The honest middle is (A) now with the gap written into docs/custos-questions.md as a known limitation, plus a tick for (B) after the demo — because (B) costs a law change, a record regeneration and a beat the script does not budget for, sixteen days out. But this is his call and beat 22 cannot be built until it is made: under (A) I build it as scripted, under (B) I need to know whether to add the reissue beat.

ADJACENT: a second finding from the same probe, smaller and not forked. An endorsement citing a REVOKED credential is committed and then not counted, whereas an endorsement citing a credential whose issuee the endorser is not is refused at commitment (beat 14). Two failure modes of one citation, landing in two different planes. That is defensible — the toolchain checks the relation, the fold checks standing at a coordinate — and it is worth one sentence of narration at beat 19 rather than leaving a viewer to notice it.

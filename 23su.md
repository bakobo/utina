THE OPERATION IS ONE LINE: src/utina/acme/build.py delegates the seat from the gAID (substrate.delegate(aids[GAID], SEAT)); the certification build plan ruled it should be substrate.delegate(aids[NINA], SEAT) instead.

WHY IT WAS NOT DONE. The plan cited custos-4.2.md:2145 for it, but :2139 two sentences earlier says 'Seated organs SHOULD be delegated identifiers of the gAID' -- a BCP-14 SHOULD whose stated reasons include that it places custodial recovery of a compromised organ inside KERI's delegated-recovery rules. Beat 20 IS a compromised organ, so that is the demo's own story. And :2145's 'the organ's AID as issuee' sits in the seat-CREDENTIAL sentence and says nothing about who the AID is delegated from.

WHAT IT BUYS AND COSTS. Delegation governs ESTABLISHMENT, not signing: a delegated AID signs with its own keys and the delegator only seals rotations. So delegating from Nina commits 'Nina controls this AID's establishment' and still does not commit 'Nina signs with it' -- less accountability than the plan's wording implies -- while the cost is total: Acme loses any key-tier lever against a stolen seat key, leaving only revoking the seat credential.

WHAT ALREADY LANDED, so a later reader does not think the plan item was missed wholesale: the COIA alias reads nina-board-seat-3 (commit 2366ce1) and the device hangs off the seat rather than off Acme (0f7b1b3). The delegator is the only fragment outstanding.

COST TO CHANGE NOW. M6 pinned the artifacts, so moving it means a full regeneration of all four tracked artifacts plus both oracles' position tables. It was free before that.
